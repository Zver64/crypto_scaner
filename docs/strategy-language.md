# Strategy language

Reference for writing a strategy expression that the scanner imports unchanged.

## Indicators and calls

- An indicator is read through a call whose first argument is the period: `<type>[_<output>]("<period>"[, parameter…])`, such as `rsi("1h")`, `rsi("1d", 21)`, `sma("1h", 20, volume)`, or `bbands_upperband("1h", 20, 2.5)`. Periods: `"1h"`, `"1d"`, `"1w"`, `"1M"`. Every call names its period; a strategy has none of its own and runs on the finest period its rules read.
- Indicators are TA-Lib functions. Types, parameter order, defaults, and output names are in `backend/internal/indicator/talib/functions_gen.go` (`indicatorType`, `params`, `outputs`). TA-Lib defaults are not obvious: `ema`, `sma`, `max`, and `min` use 30, `roc` 10, `bbands` 5.
- Parameters follow the period in that order; trailing ones may be left out and take their defaults, but one before a parameter that is set must be written even at its default: `rsi("1h")` is RSI 14, `bbands_upperband("1h", 20, 2.5)` raises the upper band, `bbands_lowerband("1h", 20, 2, 2.5)` lowers the lower one, `sma("1h", 30, volume)` is the SMA 30 of the volume. Parameters are numbers written as literals, never expressions; the MA type is a number too (`0` SMA, `1` EMA, …).
- The candle field an indicator reads (`source`) is a bare word: `open`, `high`, `low`, `close`, `volume`, `quote_volume`, `trades`, such as `max("1h", 12, high)`.
- An output may be empty on a candle: calculated, with nothing there, such as `pivot_high("1h")` on a candle that confirms no pivot. Empty is not unknown, which is a value that cannot be calculated yet. Any comparison with an empty value is false, so `!(pivot_high("1h") > 0)` is true on a candle without a pivot high, and arithmetic with an empty value is empty. An empty take profit or stop loss is unknown. Empty values are not drawn and are left out of tables and backtest values.
- An indicator with several outputs is read through one of them, whose name follows the type in the function name: `bbands_upperband("1h", 20, 2.5)`, `macd_macdsignal("1h")`, `stoch_slowk("1h")`; the bare type, such as `bbands("1h")`, is an error. An indicator with one output is called by its type: `rsi("1h")`.
- Candle fields are calls with the period only: `open`, `high`, `low`, `close`, `volume`, `quote_volume`, `trades`, such as `close("1h")`.
- A call has one canonical spelling: trailing defaults dropped and numbers without trailing zeros, so `rsi("1h", 14)` is `rsi("1h")` and `bbands_upperband("1h", 20, 2.50)` is `bbands_upperband("1h", 20, 2.5)`. Backtest values and columns, validation, and `scanner vars` use it.
- Strategies read any indicator, configured or not. Configuring one only shows it in the market table and on charts and offers it in the Mini App builder; nothing links strategies and configured indicators. Do not use `__` in names.
- Two indicators are not TA-Lib functions; they find pivots the way TradingView does. A pivot high is a value at least as high as the `left` candles before it and higher than the `right` candles after it, so of equal highs the latest counts; a pivot low is the reverse. A pivot is confirmed `right` candles after it, and only then appears.
  - `pivot` (`left`, `right`, defaults 5 and 5) reads the high and the low and has two outputs: `pivot_high("1h")` is the price of a pivot high on the candle that confirms it and empty on every other candle, and `pivot_low("1h")` likewise. So `pivot_high("1h") > 0` is true exactly on the candles confirming a pivot high, and the pivot candle lies `right` candles back. Earlier pivots are read through `ValueWhen` and `BarsSince` (see Expressions). Lower highs: `ValueWhen(pivot_high("1h")) < ValueWhen(pivot_high("1h"), 1)`.
  - `divergence` is TradingView's RSI Divergence Indicator (`period`, `left`, `right`, `range_lower`, `range_upper`, defaults 14, 5, 5, 5, 60). It finds pivots of the RSI of the close and compares the price at each with the previous pivot when `range_lower` to `range_upper` candles lie between them: the low at pivot lows, the high at pivot highs, whether or not the price has a pivot there. Its outputs are 1 on the candle confirming the later pivot and 0 elsewhere: `bull` (the price makes a lower low, the RSI a higher one), `hidden_bull` (a higher low, the RSI a lower one), `bear` (a higher high, the RSI a lower one), `hidden_bear` (a lower high, the RSI a higher one): `divergence_bull("1h") > 0`. On a candle marking a divergence of the lows (`bull` or `hidden_bull`), `previous_low_bars` counts the candles back to the earlier pivot; `previous_high_bars` does the same for the highs (`bear` or `hidden_bear`). They are 0 on every other candle, and charts do not draw them. The later pivot always lies `right` candles back. With `Prev`, a rule reads values at both pivots. The SMA 3 of the volume one candle after a pivot averages the pivot and its two neighbors, so with the default `right` of 5 that of the later pivot is `Prev(sma("1h", 3, volume), 4)`. The volume falling from the earlier pivot to the later one: `divergence_bull("1h") > 0 && Prev(sma("1h", 3, volume), divergence_previous_low_bars("1h") - 1) > Prev(sma("1h", 3, volume), 4)`.

## Expressions

- The result is a boolean: comparisons (`<`, `<=`, `>`, `>=`) of numbers and calls combined with `&&`, `||`, and `!`. Arithmetic: `+ - * /`, `Abs(x)`, `Mod(a, b)`, `Min(…)`, `Max(…)` (2–10 arguments). Functions of the language are capitalized; indicators and candle fields are lowercase, so `max("1h", 12, high)` is the indicator and `Max(a, b)` the larger value. There is no `==`, ternary, other function, string, or list.
- `Prev(x[, n])` is `x` `n` closed candles earlier (default 1, from 1 to 500); `x` may be an expression. `n` may also be calculated, then from 0 (the latest candle) to 500, by adding and subtracting whole numbers and counts of candles: `BarsSince`, the `*_bars` outputs of `divergence`, and, in the exit rule, `bars_held`, such as `Prev(close("1h"), BarsSince(pivot_low("1h")) + 5)`, the close at the latest pivot low with the default `right` of 5. A calculated shift is read at the latest candle, and only that one earlier `x` is read. It counts candles of the period of the values inside `Prev`, whatever the period it was read on: in an hourly strategy, `Prev(close("1d"), bars_held)` reads `close("1d")` `bars_held` days back. Such a `Prev` cannot contain another one, `Percentile`, `ValueWhen`, or `BarsSince` (except `BarsSince` in its shift), and those cannot contain it. A `Prev` with a number or a crossing around it is allowed and reads the shift at that earlier candle too: `Prev(Prev(close("1h"), BarsSince(pivot_low("1h")) + 5), 1)` reads `BarsSince(pivot_low("1h"))` one candle back.
- An empty calculated shift, such as a `BarsSince` that found nothing, makes the `Prev` empty. Any other calculated shift outside 0 to 500 at a candle is an error of the rule where it decides the result (`close("1h") < 0 && Prev(…)` is simply false when the close is not negative). A backtest then fails, naming the candle, the rule, and the shift; a live strategy is disabled, and, as with a manual disable, its open trade is forgotten without a sell, and the administrator gets a Telegram message saying why. A strategy whose rules were changed meanwhile stays enabled.
- `Percentile(x, n, p)` is the p-th percentile (a whole number from 1 to 100) of `x` over the `n` candles (at most 500) before the latest. Empty values are ignored: it is the percentile of the values that are not empty, and empty when all are.
- `ValueWhen(cond, x, n)` is `x` at the candle where the condition `cond` was true for the `n`-th time back, among the latest 200 closed candles, the latest included; `n` is a whole number from 0 (the latest time) to 199: `ValueWhen(rsi("1h") > 70, close("1h"), 0)`. `ValueWhen(x[, n])` is short for the condition "`x` is not empty", the `n`-th latest value of `x` (default 0): `ValueWhen(pivot_high("1h"))` is the latest pivot high and `ValueWhen(pivot_high("1h"), 1)` the one before.
- `BarsSince(cond)` is the candles since `cond` was last true among the same 200 candles, 0 when it is true at the latest one. `BarsSince(x)` is short for "`x` is not empty": `BarsSince(pivot_high("1h"))` counts the candles since a pivot high was last confirmed, so the pivot candle lies `BarsSince(pivot_high("1h")) + right` back.
- In `ValueWhen` and `BarsSince`, the argument is a condition when it is a comparison, a crossing, or `&&`, `||`, `!` over them, and a value otherwise. A condition that is unknown or empty at a candle is not true there. The result is empty when the occurrence is not among the 200 candles, and unknown when a candle that had to be looked at on the way to it is unknown. They cannot contain each other, `Percentile`, or a `Prev` with a calculated shift, and `Percentile` cannot contain them; a `Prev` with a number is allowed inside and around: `ValueWhen(pivot_low("1h") > 0, Prev(low("1h"), 5), 1)`, `Prev(BarsSince(pivot_low("1h")), 1)`.
- `CrossesAbove(a, b)` / `CrossesBelow(a, b)` is a crossing at the latest candle: `Prev(a) <= Prev(b) && a > b`, and the reverse.
- `Of("BTCUSDT", x)` is `x` of another coin, which must be active; `Of` cannot contain `Of`.
- Limits: 2000 characters and 20 comparisons (a crossing counts once, and so does each comparison of a condition inside `ValueWhen` or `BarsSince`).
- Everything is calculated on closed candles. MAX and MIN include the latest candle, so a breakout of the previous range is `close("1h") > Prev(max("1h", 12, high), 1)`. A division by zero is unknown, so it signals nothing.

## Signals

- A strategy is either a signal or a trading strategy, and both that and its direction are fixed when it is created. A signal has only an entry rule and the move it expects: `simple` (none in particular), `long` (the price rises), `short` (it falls), or `sideways` (it stays in a narrow range). A new signal is `simple` unless another move is chosen. It buys and sells nothing, so it has no exit rule, take profit, or stop loss; unlike a strategy, it may have a market cap range. A bearish warning such as “a drop may follow” is a `short` signal, not an entry into a trade.
- A signal fires like an entry signal: when the entry rule turns from false to true at a candle close, on each coin chosen for it, on each active coin among the administrator's favorites, whatever they are at the time, or on every active coin, on the finest interval its expressions read. A coin newly added to the favorites starts silently: its first evaluated candle sets its state without an alert, even when the rule is already true. A Telegram alert is titled with the signal, its interval, and the expected move, followed by the signal's message, if any, and a line per coin with its links, its close as `price`, and its `range`: the exceedance percentile 80 of the ranges (high − low) / open of exactly the latest 24 closed hourly candles, `n/a` when those are incomplete. The message when a signal is enabled lists its coins the same way.
- A `simple` signal is never judged: its backtest only counts its signals beside the evaluated candles and lists each one with its close and indicator values, and it has no window or target ratio.
- Any other signal has a window, 3, 6, 12, or 24 candles of its interval (6 by default), and a target ratio, 2, 3, 4, or 5 stops (2 by default); only its backtest reads them. The backtest judges the close of each signal candle, and of every evaluated candle alike, by a stop and a target over the window candles after it. The stop lies one ATR(14) away from the close against the expected move: the Wilder-smoothed true range, including shadows and gaps, over the shared indicator warm-up window up to and including the signal candle. The distance is independent of the window, which only limits the evaluation time; in the response it is a fraction of the signal close. The target lies the target ratio times as far in the expected direction; a `sideways` signal has a target on both sides and no stop. A `long` or `short` signal succeeds when the target is reached (by the high or the low, touching counts) before the stop; a candle reaching both fails unless its open is already at one of them. A `sideways` signal succeeds when the price touches neither target. The move to target is the largest move from the close in the expected direction, also after the target, until the candle reaching the stop (only its open counts) or the end of the window; for `sideways`, the largest move either way. Signals count only at least a window after the previous counted one; the others are repeats. The share of successful counted signals and their median move to target come beside the same over every evaluated candle of the coin: a signal is worth something only when it succeeds more often than the usual candle. Outcomes may read stored candles after the evaluated period; a candle without enough consecutive history for a positive ATR(14) (at least 14 preceding candles), or the whole consecutive window after it, is not judged. ATR restarts after a gap. For histories of at least 2,000 candles, initial evaluations also wait for the shared indicator warm-up (150 candles including the signal for ATR(14)), since older seed candles may have been pruned; shorter, complete histories need only the minimum lookback.

## Backtest charts and indicator values

- The Mini App chart is an immutable snapshot of the completed backtest, not a live chart. It shows the whole evaluated period and can scroll to its beginning, including periods longer than 2,000 candles. Stored candles before the period warm indicators up but are not displayed. Each interval shows its own closed candles and indicators; no future or live candle is appended.
- Chart dependencies cover entry, exit, take profit, and stop loss expressions, including indicators that are not configured. Display flags, configured order, and scale overrides do not determine backtest presentation. Indicators read only through `Of` are not displayed; all drawable outputs of the local dependencies are shown on their own interval. Hidden count outputs remain hidden on charts.
- The signal table adds current values of all local dependency outputs at the signal close, including hidden outputs and indicators read only through `Prev`, `Percentile`, `ValueWhen`, or `BarsSince`. Columns and values are named by canonical calls, such as `bbands_upperband("1h", 20)` or `Prev(rsi("1h"), 2)`. It does not add columns for shifted reads, function results, candle fields, or other coins. Unknown values are empty, not zero. The CLI shows these columns only with `--indicators`, and prints up to 100 latest signals or trades in text mode; `--json` returns all rows. Existing diagnostic `values` still describe the reads of the expression, independently of current indicator outputs.
- API backtests include chart snapshots only when `chart` is true (a query parameter for saved strategies, a body field for drafts). Indicator columns and current signal values are returned regardless of that option.

## Entry, exit, and trades

- Everything in this section is about trading strategies; signals only fire (see above). A trading strategy trades long or short (see Short strategies below). It has an entry rule and, optionally, an exit rule, a take profit, and a stop loss. The rules are conditions of this language; the take profit and the stop loss are prices (see below). It trades each coin chosen for it, among all active coins, on the closed candles of the finest interval its expressions read; when a coin it trades or reads through `Of` is delisted, the strategy is disabled with a Telegram message and cannot be saved or enabled until that coin is replaced or removed. Changing the coins of an enabled strategy starts it afresh, like changing its rules.
- An entry signal is the entry rule turning from false to true at a candle close. A strategy that exits, through an exit rule, a take profit, or a stop loss, holds one buy per trade: a signal opens a trade, and signals during the trade buy nothing. A strategy without any exit never sells: every entry signal buys.
- The take profit and the stop loss are price expressions evaluated at the close of the signal and fixed for the trade. A signal whose take profit is not above its close, or whose stop loss is not between 0 and its close, buys nothing. A candle that reaches the stop loss (its low at or below it) or the take profit (its high at or above it) sells the trade at that price, or at the candle's open when it opens past it; a candle that reaches both sells at the stop loss. This happens on the candle the buy fills at too.
- A signal may limit the coins it announces to a range of current market caps in USD, from CoinGecko; either bound may be absent. An entry signal on a coin outside the range, or of an unknown market cap while any bound is set, announces nothing, but the entry still counts as true, so the coin signals only after its entry turns true again within the range. Backtests ignore the range and test any coin.
- An exit signal is the exit rule being true at a candle close after the first buy. It sells every buy of the trade at the next open. The take profit and stop loss come first on their candle, and an exit wins over an entry on the same candle; after any sell the entry counts as false on that candle, so an entry still true on the next candle signals again.
- Signals are decided at the close and buys fill at the next candle's open; every buy spends the same amount. A candle is decided only once every expression it needs has its values. Live strategies catch up missed candles, such as those synchronized late or closed while the backend was down, in order like a backtest, within the synchronized 2,000 candles; alerts of such past candles name the candle's open time in UTC. A take profit or stop loss alert arrives after the close of the candle that reached it.
- Only the exit rule reads the position variables of the open trade, at the latest candle only (not inside `Prev`, `Percentile`, `ValueWhen`, `BarsSince`, crossings, or `Of`, except as a calculated `Prev` shift): `entry_price`, the average price of its buys; `pnl`, its return at the close before fees in percent (`5` is 5%); and `bars_held`, the candles since its first buy filled, counting that candle. An exit rule may read only them: `pnl >= 5 || pnl <= -3 || bars_held >= 24`.
- A price expression is arithmetic over calls and numbers with the functions above, `Prev`, `Percentile`, `ValueWhen`, `BarsSince`, and `Of`, without comparisons other than the conditions of `ValueWhen` and `BarsSince`, that reads the evaluated coin: `close("1h") * 1.05`, `bbands_upperband("1h", 20)`, `close("1h") - 2 * atr("1h")`, `Min(low("1h"), Prev(low("1h")))`. It cannot read the position variables.
- Telegram alerts announce every buy, with the take profit and stop loss of the trade, and every sell, with what sold it. Enabling or changing how a strategy trades starts it afresh, without trades; an entry true at that moment buys only once it turns true again.

## Short strategies

- A short strategy sells at the entry instead of buying and buys back at the exit, so it gains as the price falls. Everything above applies with the sides mirrored: an entry signal opens a short at the next open, and its return is `1 − exit / entry` before fees.
- A short strategy always has a take profit and a stop loss; the exit rule stays optional. So it holds one short per trade and never accumulates. The take profit must be between 0 and the close of the signal and the stop loss above it; otherwise the signal opens nothing. A candle reaching the stop loss (its high at or above it) or the take profit (its low at or below it) closes the trade at that price, or at the open when it opens past it, the stop loss first when it reaches both.
- `pnl` is the return of the short at the close before fees: `5` when the price fell 5% below `entry_price`.
- Telegram alerts announce every short, with its take profit and stop loss, and every cover, with what closed it.
- A short backtest shows the bare price move: it pays the same fee on both sides but does not model funding, leverage, or liquidation, except that a trade loses at most 100% however far the price rose, and it has no Buy & Hold or DCA baselines.
