# Strategy language

Reference for writing a strategy expression that the scanner imports unchanged.

## Indicators and variable names

- Indicators are TA-Lib functions. Types, parameter order, defaults, and output names are in `backend/internal/indicator/talib/functions_gen.go` (`indicatorType`, `params`, `outputs`). TA-Lib defaults are not obvious: `ema`, `sma`, `max`, and `min` use 30, `roc` 10, `bbands` 5.
- Two indicators are not TA-Lib functions; they find pivots the way TradingView does. A pivot high is a value at least as high as the `left` candles before it and higher than the `right` candles after it, so of equal highs the latest counts; a pivot low is the reverse. A pivot is confirmed `right` candles after it, and only then appears.
  - `pivot` (`left`, `right`, defaults 5 and 5) reads the high and the low and gives the confirmed pivot high and low and the one before each, within the latest 500 candles (otherwise the value is unknown): `h_pivot_high`, `h_pivot_previous_high`, `h_pivot_low`, `h_pivot_previous_low`. Lower highs: `h_pivot_high < h_pivot_previous_high`. `high_bars`, `previous_high_bars`, `low_bars`, and `previous_low_bars` count the candles from the latest candle back to each of those pivots and are known exactly when the pivot is (`h_pivot_low_bars`). Charts do not draw them; as counts of candles, they may be a calculated `prev` shift (see Expressions).
  - `divergence` is TradingView's RSI Divergence Indicator (`period`, `left`, `right`, `range_lower`, `range_upper`, defaults 14, 5, 5, 5, 60). It finds pivots of the RSI of the close and compares the price at each with the previous pivot when `range_lower` to `range_upper` candles lie between them: the low at pivot lows, the high at pivot highs, whether or not the price has a pivot there. Its outputs are 1 on the candle confirming the later pivot and 0 elsewhere: `bull` (the price makes a lower low, the RSI a higher one), `hidden_bull` (a higher low, the RSI a lower one), `bear` (a higher high, the RSI a lower one), `hidden_bear` (a lower high, the RSI a higher one): `h_divergence_bull > 0`. On a candle marking a divergence of the lows (`bull` or `hidden_bull`), `previous_low_bars` counts the candles back to the earlier pivot; `previous_high_bars` does the same for the highs (`bear` or `hidden_bear`). They are 0 on every other candle, and charts do not draw them. The later pivot always lies `right` candles back. With `prev`, a rule reads values at both pivots. The SMA 3 of the volume one candle after a pivot averages the pivot and its two neighbors, so with the default `right` of 5 that of the later pivot is `prev(h_sma_3_volume, 4)`. The volume falling from the earlier pivot to the later one: `h_divergence_bull > 0 && prev(h_sma_3_volume, h_divergence_previous_low_bars - 1) > prev(h_sma_3_volume, 4)`.
- An indicator title is `<interval>-<type>[-parameters]`. Intervals: `h` (1h), `d` (1d), `w` (1w), `m` (1M). Numeric parameters follow in order up to the last non-default one; trailing defaults are dropped: `h-rsi` is RSI 14, `h-rsi-21`, `h-bbands-20-2.5` (upper band), `h-bbands-20-2-2.5` (lower band). The MA type is a number (`0` SMA, `1` EMA, …).
- The candle field (`source`) is spelled as a word and only when it is not `close`: `h-max-12-high`, `h-sma-20-volume`. Fields: `open`, `high`, `low`, `close`, `volume`, `quote_asset_volume`, `trade_count`.
- A variable is the title with `.` → `p` and other characters → `_`: `h-bbands-20-2.5` → `h_bbands_20_2p5`. An indicator with several outputs appends `_<output>`: `h_bbands_20_2p5_upperband`, `h_macd_macdsignal`, `h_stoch_slowk`.
- Candle fields need no indicator: `<interval>_open`, `_high`, `_low`, `_close`, `_volume`, `_quote_volume`, `_trades` (`h_close`).
- A name must spell back exactly one indicator. Strategies read indicators that are not configured without adding them; the Mini App adds them only before showing the rules in the builder. Do not invent names or use `__`.

## Expressions

- The result is a boolean: comparisons (`<`, `<=`, `>`, `>=`) of numbers and variables combined with `&&`, `||`, and `!`. Arithmetic: `+ - * /`, `abs(x)`, `mod(a, b)`, `min(…)`, `max(…)` (2–10 arguments). There is no `==`, ternary, other function, string, or list.
- `prev(x[, n])` is `x` `n` closed candles earlier (default 1, from 1 to 500); `x` may be an expression. `n` may also be calculated by adding and subtracting whole numbers and counts of candles: the `*_bars` outputs of `pivot` and `divergence` and, in the exit rule, `bars_held`, such as `prev(h_close, h_pivot_low_bars - 1)`. A calculated shift is read at the latest candle, and only that one earlier `x` is read. It counts candles of the interval of the values inside `prev`, whatever the interval it was read on: in an hourly strategy, `prev(d_close, bars_held)` reads `d_close` `bars_held` days back. Such a `prev` cannot contain another one or `percentile`, and `percentile` cannot contain it. A `prev` with a number or a crossing around it is allowed and reads the shift at that earlier candle too: `prev(prev(h_close, h_pivot_low_bars), 1)` reads `h_pivot_low_bars` one candle back.
- A calculated shift outside 1 to 500 at a candle is an error of the rule where it decides the result (`h_close < 0 && prev(…)` is simply false when the close is not negative). A backtest then fails, naming the candle, the rule, and the shift; a live strategy is disabled, and, as with a manual disable, its open trade is forgotten without a sell, and the administrator gets a Telegram message saying why. A strategy whose rules were changed meanwhile stays enabled.
- `percentile(x, n, p)` is the p-th percentile (0–100) of `x` over the `n` candles (at most 500) before the latest.
- `crosses_above(a, b)` / `crosses_below(a, b)` is a crossing at the latest candle: `prev(a) <= prev(b) && a > b`, and the reverse.
- `of("BTCUSDT", x)` is `x` of another coin, which must be in the administrator's favorites; `of` cannot contain `of`.
- Limits: 2000 characters and 20 comparisons (a crossing counts once).
- Everything is calculated on closed candles. MAX and MIN include the latest candle, so a breakout of the previous range is `h_close > prev(h_max_12_high, 1)`. A division by zero is unknown, so it signals nothing.

## Signals

- A strategy is either a signal or a trading strategy, and both that and its direction are fixed when it is created. A signal has only an entry rule and the move it expects: `long` (the price rises), `short` (it falls), or `sideways` (it stays in a narrow range). It buys and sells nothing, so it has no exit rule, take profit, or stop loss; like a strategy, it may have a market cap range. A bearish warning such as “a drop may follow” is a `short` signal, not an entry into a trade.
- A signal fires like an entry signal: when the entry rule turns from false to true at a candle close, on every coin in the administrator's favorites, on the finest interval its expressions read. A Telegram alert names the signal, the coin, the expected move, and the close, then the rule and its values or the message.
- A signal has a window, 3, 6, 12, or 24 candles of its interval (6 by default), and a target ratio, 2, 3, 4, or 5 stops (2 by default); only its backtest reads them. The backtest judges the close of each signal candle, and of every evaluated candle alike, by a stop and a target over the window candles after it. The stop lies one ATR(14) away from the close against the expected move: the Wilder-smoothed true range, including shadows and gaps, up to and including the signal candle. The distance is independent of the window, which only limits the evaluation time; in the response it is a fraction of the signal close. The target lies the target ratio times as far in the expected direction; a `sideways` signal has a target on both sides and no stop. A `long` or `short` signal succeeds when the target is reached (by the high or the low, touching counts) before the stop; a candle reaching both fails unless its open is already at one of them. A `sideways` signal succeeds when the price touches neither target. The move to target is the largest move from the close in the expected direction, also after the target, until the candle reaching the stop (only its open counts) or the end of the window; for `sideways`, the largest move either way. Signals count only at least a window after the previous counted one; the others are repeats. The share of successful counted signals and their median move to target come beside the same over every evaluated candle of the coin: a signal is worth something only when it succeeds more often than the usual candle. Outcomes may read stored candles after the evaluated period; a candle without enough consecutive history for a positive ATR(14) (at least 14 preceding candles), or the whole consecutive window after it, is not judged. ATR restarts after a gap. For histories of at least 2,000 candles, initial evaluations also wait for the shared indicator warm-up (150 candles including the signal for ATR(14)), since older seed candles may have been pruned; shorter, complete histories need only the minimum lookback.

## Entry, exit, and trades

- Everything in this section is about trading strategies; signals only fire (see above). A trading strategy trades long or short (see Short strategies below). It has an entry rule and, optionally, an exit rule, a take profit, and a stop loss. The rules are conditions of this language; the take profit and the stop loss are prices (see below). It trades every coin in the administrator's favorites on the closed candles of the finest interval its expressions read.
- An entry signal is the entry rule turning from false to true at a candle close. A strategy that exits, through an exit rule, a take profit, or a stop loss, holds one buy per trade: a signal opens a trade, and signals during the trade buy nothing. A strategy without any exit never sells: every entry signal buys.
- The take profit and the stop loss are price expressions evaluated at the close of the signal and fixed for the trade. A signal whose take profit is not above its close, or whose stop loss is not between 0 and its close, buys nothing. A candle that reaches the stop loss (its low at or below it) or the take profit (its high at or above it) sells the trade at that price, or at the candle's open when it opens past it; a candle that reaches both sells at the stop loss. This happens on the candle the buy fills at too.
- A strategy or a signal may limit the coins it buys or announces to a range of current market caps in USD, from CoinGecko; either bound may be absent. An entry signal on a coin outside the range, or of an unknown market cap while any bound is set, buys or announces nothing, but the entry still counts as true, so the coin buys or signals only after its entry turns true again within the range. Open trades sell as usual. Backtests ignore the range and trade any coin.
- An exit signal is the exit rule being true at a candle close after the first buy. It sells every buy of the trade at the next open. The take profit and stop loss come first on their candle, and an exit wins over an entry on the same candle; after any sell the entry counts as false on that candle, so an entry still true on the next candle signals again.
- Signals are decided at the close and buys fill at the next candle's open; every buy spends the same amount. A candle is decided only once every expression it needs has its values. Live alerts check only the latest closed candle, so candles that close while the backend is down are skipped; a take profit or stop loss alert arrives after the close of the candle that reached it.
- Only the exit rule reads the position variables of the open trade, at the latest candle only (not inside `prev`, `percentile`, crossings, or `of`, except as a calculated `prev` shift): `entry_price`, the average price of its buys; `pnl`, its return at the close before fees in percent (`5` is 5%); and `bars_held`, the candles since its first buy filled, counting that candle. An exit rule may read only them: `pnl >= 5 || pnl <= -3 || bars_held >= 24`.
- A price expression is arithmetic over variables and numbers with the functions above, `prev`, `percentile`, and `of`, without comparisons, that reads the evaluated coin: `h_close * 1.05`, `h_bbands_20_2_2_upperband`, `h_close - 2 * h_atr_14`, `min(h_low, prev(h_low))`. It cannot read the position variables.
- Telegram alerts announce every buy, with the take profit and stop loss of the trade, and every sell, with what sold it. Enabling or changing how a strategy trades starts it afresh, without trades; an entry true at that moment buys only once it turns true again.

## Short strategies

- A short strategy sells at the entry instead of buying and buys back at the exit, so it gains as the price falls. Everything above applies with the sides mirrored: an entry signal opens a short at the next open, and its return is `1 − exit / entry` before fees.
- A short strategy always has a take profit and a stop loss; the exit rule stays optional. So it holds one short per trade and never accumulates. The take profit must be between 0 and the close of the signal and the stop loss above it; otherwise the signal opens nothing. A candle reaching the stop loss (its high at or above it) or the take profit (its low at or below it) closes the trade at that price, or at the open when it opens past it, the stop loss first when it reaches both.
- `pnl` is the return of the short at the close before fees: `5` when the price fell 5% below `entry_price`.
- Telegram alerts announce every short, with its take profit and stop loss, and every cover, with what closed it.
- A short backtest shows the bare price move: it pays the same fee on both sides but does not model funding, leverage, or liquidation, except that a trade loses at most 100% however far the price rose, and it has no Buy & Hold or DCA baselines.
